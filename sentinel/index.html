The problem, in your units

When a plant gets hit, nobody counts files. They count shifts.

Your MSP will hand you a report about encrypted records. Your foreman will tell you the line has been down since Tuesday and the customer wants to know about Friday's order. Those are two different problems, and most security vendors only understand one of them.

If any of these are true, we should talk

  • You've never watched a backup actually restore. You've only seen the green checkmark.
  • Your "IT department" is a break-fix vendor and whoever on staff is best with computers.
  • Your insurance renewal now asks security questions nobody can answer.
  • Something already happened, and you're not confident it's over.

What I don't do

“Can you tell us who did this?” No, and in Texas I'm not permitted to.

Determining who is responsible for an incident, or preparing findings for a court, insurance or other proceeding, is licensed investigative work under Chapter 1702 of the Occupations Code, and I don't hold that license. Response and hardening are a different thing, and the same statute says so directly: reviewing computer-based data to prepare for or respond to a cybersecurity event is expressly outside the licensing requirement (Tex. Occ. Code §1702.104(c)).

If what you need crosses that line, I'll say so on the first call and refer you to someone licensed. Everything I've already captured goes with you.

Straight answers

The questions everybody asks.

“We're too small to be a target.”+

Small shops aren't targeted, they're swept up. Automated scanning doesn't check your headcount before it tries your remote-access port.

“We already have an IT guy.”+

Good. Keep him. This isn't help desk work. It's the layer above it, and most break-fix vendors will tell you the same thing if you ask them directly.

“Isn't this going to be a huge project?”+

The Walkthrough is half a day and a fixed fee. Start there. If the findings say you're in decent shape, that's a real outcome and we're done.

“Can you guarantee we won't get hit?”+

No, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. What I can do is make sure you know what you're exposed to, that your backups actually restore, and that you have a plan for the day it matters.
